How to turn an Instagram reel into text, including the words on screen

Inam Ul Haq·Updated September 22, 2026·8 min read
The short answer

You cannot copy a transcript directly from Instagram. The app only shows closed captions at the bottom of the screen while a reel plays. If you want to turn a reel into text, you have to transcribe the audio yourself using a public reel link or a saved file. Many reels show their main points as text on the screen, and audio transcribers miss that completely, so you need to keep pictures of the screen too.

You might see a reel sharing a recipe, an app list, or a money tip. You want to save those words as text to search later or paste into ChatGPT. Instagram simply does not offer a button to do that. And if you run the video through a free reel transcriber, you sometimes get an almost empty text file, even when the reel had a lot of useful details.

An audio transcriber only listens to the spoken sound. Many reels put their actual information on the screen as text over background music, and the speaker just says something simple like "here are my favourites". So if you want to turn a reel into text properly, you need the spoken words and you also need the words printed on the screen.

Does Instagram give you a transcript of a reel?

Instagram has closed captions, which means their system listens to the audio and writes the words at the bottom of the video as it plays. But you cannot copy this text. The Instagram help page on managing closed captions for reels explains that the video creator controls this feature. That page also mentions this setting only works in the Instagram app, and it is not available on computers.

The captions just flash on screen for you to read along, and then they disappear. You will not find a panel showing the full text like the Show transcript button under YouTube videos. The paragraph written below the reel is completely different, because it is just the caption the creator typed when posting, and it is often a joke, a summary, or hashtags instead of the actual words spoken.

Instagram will not give you the text directly, so you must transcribe the audio yourself using the video link or the saved file.

What is the free way to turn a reel into text?

Instagram might let you save the reel if it comes from a public account. Their official guide on who can download your reels points out that anyone can download a public reel unless the creator disables that option. When you have the downloaded file on your computer, you can run a free transcription without uploading the video anywhere.

  1. On the reel, tap the share arrow. If the creator allows it, you will see Download. Tap it and the reel saves to your phone.
  2. Move the file to your computer, the same way you move any video from your phone.
  3. Open VideoDoc's free browser version and drop the file in. The words are transcribed inside your own browser and the video is not uploaded.
  4. Copy the text, or paste it into ChatGPT together with your question.

If you only need the spoken words from one public reel, the free link transcribers you find on Google will work fine. You just give them the reel link, and their servers fetch the video to process it. Since the reel is already public, you do not really need to worry about privacy in this case.

This free method has a few hard limits. You cannot download reels from private accounts at all. A creator can also disable downloads, and the accounts of people under 18 have downloads turned off by default. Any file you save includes an Instagram watermark showing the username, and the company states you cannot use downloaded reels for commercial work. Finally, the free browser version only gives you the text, with no pictures of the screen.

Why does a reel transcript miss half of what the reel says?

A transcript only captures the sound, but many creators design their reels for people watching with the volume off. They type the important details right on the video, like a list, a price, a setting, or a step, and leave the audio as just music or a short sentence. A transcription tool cannot write down words that no one actually said.

If the video shows text on the screen, you need to save pictures of the screen along with the audio transcript.

Saving pictures helps because AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude can read text printed inside an image. A document that holds the transcript and the screen pictures gives your AI the complete message of the reel. This works even though the AI cannot watch the video itself.

How do I get the spoken words and the on-screen text together?

I built VideoDoc to handle this exact problem. It is a desktop application that runs on your own computer, supporting Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. It takes a video and creates a single PDF and Markdown document holding the timestamped words and pictures of what was on the screen. The steps are very short for a public reel.

  1. In Instagram, tap the share arrow and then Copy link.
  2. Paste the link into VideoDoc. It sees that the link is from Instagram, and you choose A document for AI.
  3. VideoDoc downloads the reel and listens to the audio on your computer. Instagram has no caption file to fetch, so it always transcribes the sound, which takes a little longer than a YouTube video that has captions.
  4. You get the transcript with a timestamp on every line, plus pictures of the screen, each with its own timestamp.
  5. Give the PDF to ChatGPT or Claude and ask it to write out the on-screen text as well. VideoDoc keeps that text as pictures. It does not type it out for you.

I had to build a specific fix for vertical video. A normal frame from a reel is tall and thin. If you keep it at full size, it cannot fit on a standard A4 page, and this can break the PDF layout. VideoDoc solves this by shrinking tall pictures to fit the page cleanly, and it places the timestamp right below every image.

You might want to capture every single text card instead of just the key moments. In that case, you should pick the Every frame as images option. It saves the video as a series of pictures at 1, 2 or 4 a second, and it automatically drops any picture that exactly matches the previous one. A 60 second reel at 4 a second produces 240 pictures before the tool removes those duplicates.

A reel that requires an account login works a bit differently. VideoDoc is designed for public videos, so it will stop and warn you when a reel asks for a login. If you are viewing your own reel, or if Instagram gives you the Download option, you should save the video file directly. You can then drop that local file into VideoDoc, which always works and keeps all processing on your computer.

Honest limits

Since VideoDoc focuses on public reels, a reel from a private account will simply not open by link. A reel that just plays music gives you an empty transcript, because there are no spoken words to hear. The text on the screen is saved as pictures, so you or your AI still have to read them. Automated transcription can also struggle with slang, very fast talking, or voices buried under loud music, so you should always check names and numbers against the actual reel before you trust them.

Quick questions

Can you get a transcript of an Instagram reel?

Instagram does not offer this directly. The app shows closed captions at the bottom of the screen while the video plays, but there is no complete text block you can copy. You have to transcribe the audio yourself using the public reel link or the downloaded video file.

Why is my Instagram reel transcript empty?

The video probably uses background music and text on the screen instead of spoken words. A transcription tool only processes audio, so it completely misses information that is written visually. You need to save pictures of the screen to read that text.

Can I turn a reel from a private account into text?

You cannot do this using a web link. Instagram blocks downloads for private accounts, and VideoDoc is built to handle public videos only. But if you have the actual video file, like a reel you made yourself, you can simply load it into the tool as a local file.

Can I turn many reels into text at once?

Yes, VideoDoc lets you paste multiple public reel links, putting one on each line. You can also load a .txt file full of links, and the software will create a separate document for each reel.
