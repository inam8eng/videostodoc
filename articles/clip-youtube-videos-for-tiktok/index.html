How to clip YouTube videos for TikTok the right way

Inam Ul Haq·Updated November 20, 2026·7 min read
The short answer

Yes, you can clip a YouTube video for TikTok if the video is yours, or if the creator gave you permission in writing. Cut the best part into a tall 1080x1920 frame, add captions, and include something of your own, because TikTok keeps reused videos with nothing new out of its For You feed. A free phone editor is enough when you only want to make one clip.

You have a long YouTube video, maybe your own podcast or a stream from GTA 6 launch week, and you want to share short pieces of it on TikTok. Most pages that answer this question are converter tools, so they jump straight to pasting a link. The real first question is whether you are allowed to post that clip at all.

If it is your own video, yes. If it is someone else's video, only with their written permission. Even when you have permission, the clip still needs something new from you, because TikTok and YouTube both treat a plain copy as reused content. I will explain that rule first and then walk through the steps, including a free method that needs no extra software.

Read this first

Nothing on this page is legal or financial advice. I am using GTA 6 launch streams as an example here, and that game belongs to Take-Two and Rockstar Games. This site has no connection to either company, and Rockstar can take down footage at any time. Most people who make clips earn very little at the start, and getting a streamer's permission does not make those clips eligible for YouTube ads.

Can I clip YouTube videos for TikTok?

TikTok answers the first part of this rule directly. Its Community Guidelines, which I read on 20 November 2026, state "You should only post content you created or have the right to share" (TikTok). That means the answer depends on who owns the video, and there are only three possible cases.

Getting permission is only half the job, and many people find this out too late. The same TikTok page explains that a video is left out of the For You feed (FYF, the main feed where new viewers discover your posts) when it "includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new". YouTube follows the same logic, and its channel monetization policies state that the reused content rule still applies even if the original creator gave you permission.

Permission gives you the right to post a clip, and your own addition is what makes the clip yours. That addition could be an opening line on screen explaining why the moment matters, your own voice recorded over the clip, or your own commentary on what happened. Simply cropping the video and adding captions does not count as adding something new.

Game streams bring one more layer to think about. GTA 6 came out on 19 November 2026 (Rockstar Games), so there are plenty of long streams to clip from launch week. A streamer's permission covers their voice, their face, and their commentary, but the game footage inside the stream still belongs to Rockstar, and the streamer cannot hand you rights over it. I covered what Rockstar's own policy says in what really pays from GTA 6.

What do you need before you cut?

To make the clip, you need the video and a tool that turns a wide picture into a tall frame. I build a desktop app called VideoDoc, and its Video to shorts tab is built for this task, so I will use it to show the steps. You can follow the same steps in any editor.

The tab accepts a video in three ways: you can drop the file in, paste the path of a file stored on your computer, or paste a public YouTube link. Using a local file is the safer option for a long stream because the app does not have to download anything first. Any link you paste must be public, so a private or members only video has to be a file that the owner sends you.

The app processes the audio on your own computer, and the video is not uploaded anywhere. The first time you run it, the app downloads its speech engine once, which takes 486 MB for the Balanced option. Lite, the free version, handles videos up to 20 minutes long and adds a small "Made with VideoDoc" mark to each clip. Pro removes both the time limit and the mark.

How do you cut the clip, step by step?

  1. Add the video. Drop the file, paste its path, or paste the public link of a video you have the right to use.
  2. Read the list of best parts. Each part has 1 to 5 dots and a plain reason beside it, for example that it opens with a question or that it is a complete thought. The list is sorted into four lengths, the shortest up to 30 seconds and the longest 2 to 3 minutes.
  3. Watch each one before you trust it. Treat the dots as a shortlist, because the app cannot tell whether the joke lands or what the moment means in the game.
  4. Trim it on the timeline. The handles snap to whole words, so no word is cut in half.
  5. Pick the frame. It can follow the speaker's face, show the whole picture, or stack the facecam above the screen.
  6. Read the captions once. They are word by word and switched on from the start, and a speech engine gets names and slang wrong more often than anything else.
  7. Export at 1080x1920. That is the tall shape TikTok shows on a phone, and the clip is saved as a file on your computer.
  8. Add your own part and upload it yourself. VideoDoc does not post to TikTok. Put your first line or your voice on the clip, then upload it from your own account.

The final step is what decides whether your clip counts as your own work or just a copy. I would spend more time writing that opening line than adjusting the crop.

What is the free way, and where does it stop?

You can do all of this without using my app. Move the video onto your phone, open it in TikTok's built-in editor or a free app like CapCut, trim the part you want, crop it into a tall frame, and turn on automatic captions. Which caption tools stay free changes frequently, so check what your current version includes. If you only want one or two clips from a video you already know well, this approach is plenty and I would not pay for extra software.

The limitation with a phone editor is finding the right moments. Nothing highlights the good parts for you, so working through a 3 hour stream means watching for 3 hours, or doing a lot of skipping and guessing. Transferring a large video file onto a phone is slow too. If you clip the same creator every week, finding those moments is the part worth handing over to a tool.

Quick questions

Can I post someone else's YouTube video on TikTok if I give credit?

No, mentioning the creator's name does not count as having their permission. Get a written yes from them first, and make sure you still add something of your own to the clip.

Is there a free way to clip YouTube videos for TikTok?

Yes, you can use TikTok's own editor or a free phone app to trim, crop, and caption a clip. VideoDoc Lite is also free for videos up to 20 minutes, though it places a small mark on each clip.

Does VideoDoc work from a YouTube link?

Yes, the Video to shorts tab accepts a public link, a local file, or the path to a file on your computer. A private video must be loaded as a file. Only use it on videos you are allowed to clip.
