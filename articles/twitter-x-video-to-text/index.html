How to turn a Twitter (X) video into text when there is no transcript button

Inam Ul Haq·Updated September 29, 2026·6 min read
The short answer

X has no transcript button. You cannot copy the words from a video post the way you do on YouTube. The way around it is to copy the post link and paste it into a tool that listens to the audio and writes it down with timestamps. You also need to keep pictures of the screen to save any text shown on the video. Public posts work by link, but a protected post needs the video file itself.

You see a video on X and want to keep the words. It might be a founder explaining their pricing or a doctor talking about a study or a two minute tutorial showing a setting you need. You open the menu to find a transcript and there is nothing there. So you end up scrubbing back and forth and typing the words out by hand.

X does not give you a transcript, so you have to make one from the audio. That is the whole method. Getting the words out of a post takes a minute or two once you know how to do it, and you can do the first part for free.

Why is there no transcript on X videos?

YouTube builds a text version of most videos and hides it behind a Show transcript button. X never built that panel. Some X videos do show captions on the screen. These come from automatic captions or a file the person uploaded, and X explains this in its help page on uploading a caption file.

But captions on X play over the video one line at a time and then disappear. There is no button to show them all as one block you can copy. Many posts have no captions at all, so the audio is the only place the words exist.

There is another problem that the top tools for this search never mention. Many X videos show words on screen that are never spoken out loud. You might see a chart or a code snippet or a quote card or a price table shown for a few seconds. A text transcript of the audio alone misses all of this visual information.

Is there a free way to get the words from a Twitter video?

You can do this on your own computer. Windows 11 has a feature called Live Captions. You press the Windows key and Ctrl and L together, and then play the X video. Windows writes the speech on the screen as it listens. Microsoft confirms in its Live Captions help page that all of this happens on the device and nothing goes to the cloud.

The limit is that Live Captions does not save anything. The same page says the captions are not stored anywhere, so you only read them and do not get a file. You can pause a short clip and copy the lines into a note. I think that is fine if you only have one video.

If the video is your own post, you already have the file on your phone or laptop. You can skip X completely and transcribe the file free without uploading it. This gets you real text with timestamps.

How do I turn an X video into text from the link?

I built VideoDoc for this exact part. I wanted to paste a link and get the words and the screen pictures together without sending the video to someone else's server. It works on public X posts the same way it works on TikTok and YouTube.

  1. On the post, tap the share icon and choose Copy link. On a computer you can also copy the address from the browser bar.
  2. Paste the link into VideoDoc. It sees that it is an X link as soon as you paste it, and gives you three choices: A document for AI, Every frame as images, or Just the video file.
  3. Pick A document for AI. X videos have no captions for it to borrow, so VideoDoc listens to the audio on your own computer and writes it down with timestamps. It is a little slower than YouTube, but nothing leaves your machine.
  4. Open the result. You get a PDF and a Markdown file with the words, plus pictures of the screen at the moments that matter, so a chart or a code snippet shown for three seconds is still there.

You can choose Every frame as images if you want every moment of the screen and not only the main parts. At 1 frame a second a two minute clip makes 120 pictures. VideoDoc drops any picture that is exactly the same as the one before it, so a still slide does not fill up your folder.

I work in telecom, and the X videos I actually save are engineers walking through a network diagram. The speaker in those videos says "as you can see here" again and again. The text transcript on its own makes no sense, so the pictures of the screen end up being the real content.

What if the post is private or X asks me to log in?

A post from a protected account is only visible to the people that account approved, and no link tool should try to get around that. VideoDoc works on public videos. It shows you a clear sign in message instead of a confusing error when a video needs a login.

If you cannot open the post without logging in, work from the video file instead. You might own the video or someone sent you the file. You can open it in VideoDoc as a local file and you get the same document made on your own computer.

Honest limits

Only public posts work by link. The transcript is just a machine listening, so names and slang and technical terms can come out wrong. You should check any line you plan to quote against the video. A clip that is mostly music with three spoken words only gives you three words. A text that someone posted is still their work, so you must quote it with credit and not repost it as your own.

Quick questions

Does X have a transcript for videos?

No. X has no Show transcript button like YouTube. Some videos show captions over the picture, but there is no button to see or copy them all at once.

How do I get the text from a Twitter video for free?

You can use Windows 11 and press the Windows key and Ctrl and L to turn on Live Captions, and then play the video. It writes the speech on the screen on your own computer. It does not save anything, so you need to copy what you want as you read.

Can I transcribe a video from a private X account?

You cannot do it from the link. The posts on a protected account are only for the people that account approved. You can transcribe the file instead if you have the video file and are allowed to use it.

Does it also get the text shown on screen in the video?

A normal transcript only gives you the spoken words. VideoDoc keeps pictures of the screen next to the text. This means a chart or a quote card or a code snippet shown for a few seconds is kept too.
