Turn video lectures into notes, automatically

Inam Ul Haq·Updated July 18, 2026·6 min read
The short answer

Give the lecture link or file to VideoDoc and it writes the notes for you on your own computer: every sentence timestamped, every slide captured as an image, one clean PDF and Markdown file. Your job stops being stenographer and becomes what it should be: reading, marking and questioning.

My handwritten lecture notes had a pattern. Clean and confident for the first twenty minutes, then wobbly, then a sentence that ends mid-word where I gave up and just watched. Nobody can listen, understand, and transcribe at the same time, because writing one point means missing the next one. The night before my telecom finals I had notes that covered maybe a third of what was taught, and no memory of which third.

Let the lecture write its own notes

The mechanical part of note-taking, getting the words down, is exactly what a machine should do. Point VideoDoc at the lecture, a YouTube link, a whole playlist, or a recording on your disk, and it produces the complete notes: timestamped transcript, plus every slide and worked example captured as a full HD image, placed where it appeared. It runs on your own computer, so a 3 hour lecture costs nothing and private recordings stay private.

The three pass method I use on the document

  1. Skim the captures first. Scrolling just the images gives you the lecture's skeleton in two minutes, because teachers put the structure on the slides.
  2. Read with a highlighter, not a pen. The words are already written, so your whole attention goes to marking what matters and writing tiny margin thoughts. This is the difference between recording and understanding.
  3. Let an AI quiz you. Drop the PDF into ChatGPT or Claude: "Make 10 exam questions from this lecture, then grade my answers hard." The timestamps mean every question links back to the exact minute to review.

Search is the superpower nobody mentions

One semester of lectures as documents means exam prep starts with Ctrl+F. The professor mentioned the marking scheme once, in passing, in week 4? Found in three seconds. Compare that to scrubbing through hours of video on 2x speed with your finger hovering over the pause key. That is the real trade: watching is linear, notes are random access.

Really happened

A formula I had hunted through a 3 hour lecture for twenty minutes became a single Ctrl+F after I documented it. That one search is why VideoDoc exists.

Quick questions

Can I get notes from a lecture that has no captions?

Yes. VideoDoc transcribes the audio itself on your machine, so captions are irrelevant. It handles many languages, with English the strongest today.

Do the notes include the slides?

Yes. The on-screen content is captured as images, slides and worked examples rather than the lecturer's face, and placed beside the words spoken at that moment.

Can it handle a whole semester playlist?

Yes. Give it the playlist link and choose one merged document or one per lecture. Long runs are safe: pause, resume, and a safety timer on every step.

Pick tomorrow's hardest lecture and let it write its own notes tonight. Walk in having already read them once. That feeling is worth the setup.
