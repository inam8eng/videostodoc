Turn any course video into study notes you can search

Inam Ul Haq·Updated August 27, 2026·6 min read
The short answer

Run each course video, or the whole playlist, through VideoDoc on your own computer. You get one document per lesson or one merged file for the course: timestamped words plus the slides and demos as images. Keep the Markdown in your notes app, keep the PDF for your AI, and revise by search instead of rewatch.

Be honest, how many courses have you bought and never finished? I own several. The ones I actually completed had one thing in common: I stopped trying to absorb them by watching, and started turning them into something I could hold. A course you can search is a course you will actually use, because the barrier to "let me check that lesson again" drops from twenty minutes to twenty seconds.

The conversion, once per course

If the course lives on YouTube, paste the playlist link into VideoDoc and choose one merged document or one per lesson. If it is video files you downloaded in HD or 4K, drop those in instead, and they process fully offline. Either way the work happens on your machine: transcription with timestamps, and every slide, diagram and demo extracted from the video as a sharp image beside the words. Queue it in the evening, and the tracker sheet shows you the finished list with your morning tea.

The simple system that survives

  • Markdown into your notes app. Obsidian or Notion becomes the course's permanent home, one note per lesson, linkable to your own notes. The course stops being "that thing in my downloads" and joins your actual knowledge base.
  • PDF into your AI. ChatGPT, Claude or NotebookLM with the whole course can explain lesson 9 using lesson 3's vocabulary, build you a revision plan, or make quizzes per module, which is the same reading-and-quizzing loop as studying from YouTube with AI. Ask it "what does this course never explain properly" some time. Eye-opening.
  • Timestamps as your way back. Notes are for finding, video is for re-feeling. When a captured slide is not enough, the timestamp beside it takes you to the exact minute to rewatch the demo.
The fair use line, plainly

Notes from a paid course are for you. Making personal study notes from content you bought is normal studying. Sharing those documents, selling them, or posting them publicly is sharing the course itself, so keep them private, the same as your handwritten notes.

Why this actually gets courses finished

The reason courses die at lesson 7 is friction: each return visit costs a login, a scrub, a "where was I". Notes remove the friction. You revisit in the gaps of a normal day, on the bus, between tasks, because reading two pages needs no ceremony. The course finishes almost by accident, which is the only way anything finishes when you have a day job. I say that from experience.

Quick questions

Does this work with downloaded course files, not just YouTube?

Yes. Local video files process fully offline on your own computer, which also means private course recordings never touch the internet.

Should I make one big document or one per lesson?

One per lesson for a course you will revisit selectively, one merged file when you want your AI to see the whole course at once. VideoDoc offers both, and you can run it twice for both shapes.

Is it okay to make notes from a paid course?

Personal study notes from a course you bought are normal studying. Keep them private. Sharing or selling them would be sharing the course itself.
