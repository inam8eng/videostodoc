How to turn a Kaltura lecture into notes, when the course player has no download button

Inam Ul Haq·Updated November 3, 2026·6 min read
The short answer

Kaltura videos in Canvas, Moodle or Blackboard play in a course player that is view only for students by default, so you usually will not find a download button. The free way to get notes is to open the transcript beside the player, search it, and often download it as a text file. If you need notes that include the slides, ask your lecturer to switch on the player with a download option or ask them to send you the video file.

You have a lecture in Canvas under a tab called My Media or Media Gallery, or it sits inside a Moodle or Blackboard page. The video plays fine, but you need proper notes before the exam. You look for a download button on the player and you cannot find one.

That video player is Kaltura, and a missing download button is normal. On most courses a Kaltura video is view only for students until the lecturer chooses to change it. I will explain how that setting works, how to use the free transcript most students ignore, and how to get notes with slides next to the words when you are allowed to have the file.

Why is there no download button on my Kaltura lecture?

Kaltura is a video system that many universities connect to their learning platforms. A lecturer adds a video to a course and it loads in a course player. George Mason University's Kaltura download guide explains clearly that the default player only gives students permission to view the video.

A lecturer has to choose a different player when they add the video if they want students to download it. That guide calls it the Course Player with Student Download Option. The download button appears after you start playing the video. This means the choice is made for each separate video, so one lecture on your course might have a button while the next one does not.

Do not try to bypass a download button that is turned off. Recording your screen or using a trick in your browser goes against the choice your lecturer made, and it can break the rules at your university. Asking for the file is faster than you might think, and the rest of this page focuses on using what you already have access to.

What is the free way to get notes when I cannot download it?

Kaltura can display a transcript next to the player if the video has captions. Kaltura's official transcript plugin page says you can search this text and click a word to jump to that exact moment in the video. The plugin includes a download button and a print button, but your school's administrators can turn those two buttons off, so check if you have them before you rely on them.

Downloading the transcript gives you a .txt file of the spoken words. The download will match the language you are viewing if the video has captions in multiple languages. Some schools also include an extra tool called Annoto, and Florida State's Annoto guide explains that these notes are linked to specific moments in the video, they are private to you, and you can export them.

  1. Start playing the lecture and check the player for a download icon first.
  2. Open the transcript beside the video and use the search bar to find the parts you need if the download icon is missing.
  3. You can download or print the transcript if your school leaves those buttons enabled.
  4. Paste the text into your own notes file and manually write down the minutes where an important slide appears on screen.

This method costs nothing and it gets the job done. The problem is that the transcript has no slides. A slide carries half the meaning in a normal lecture, because the lecturer will tell you to look at a curve, but the text transcript cannot show you that curve.

How do I get the slides beside the words?

Getting slides and notes together requires the actual video file. This means it only works when the download icon is active or when you ask the lecturer for the file. A short message explaining you need the video for revision is usually enough. Many lecturers simply do not know the default player is view only.

Honest limits

It cannot help with a lecture you are not permitted to download, and it should not. The free Kaltura transcript is a faster choice if your lecturer already provides the slides as a PDF and you just need the text. Turning an hour of audio into text on a standard laptop without a graphics card takes a long time.

Quick questions

Can students download Kaltura videos?

Students can only download the file when the lecturer allows it. The default course player is set to view only, and a lecturer has to embed the video using a specific player that includes a download option. Ask your lecturer for the file if you start the video and see no download icon.

How do I download a Kaltura transcript?

You can open the transcript next to the player and click its download button to save a .txt file if the video has captions. Your school administrators can turn that button off, so it will not appear on every single course.

Can I take notes on a Kaltura video?

Some schools connect a tool called Annoto to Kaltura. This allows you to type private notes that link to the exact time in the video and you can export them. If you do not have Annoto, just download the text transcript and write your own notes next to it.

Is it allowed to screen record a Kaltura lecture?

The lecturer made a choice if the download button is switched off. Recording the screen to bypass that choice can break the rules at your university. Ask the lecturer for the file directly instead.
