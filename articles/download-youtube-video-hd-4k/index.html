How to download a YouTube video in HD or 4K

Inam Ul Haq·Updated August 27, 2026·5 min read
The short answer

VideoDoc has a built-in downloader: paste the link, choose Just download the video, pick HD or 4K, and the file saves through your own home connection. It handles single videos and whole playlists, from YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and X. Download content you have the right to save: your own uploads, permitted or licensed material, and things offline viewing is allowed for.

My internet is not always my friend. Load-shedding, slow evenings, a train ride with one bar of signal. Half of why I download videos is simply that streaming assumes a connection I do not always have, and the other half is backup, because I have watched enough good videos vanish from platforms to stop trusting "it will be there later".

The download itself, three clicks

  1. Paste the link into VideoDoc. YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook or X, it detects the platform on its own.
  2. Choose Just download the video instead of Make a document. Both options sit side by side, and it is genuinely fine to use VideoDoc as only a downloader.
  3. Pick the quality, up to full HD or 4K when the source has it, and press Start. The file lands in your output folder.

Playlists work the same way: paste the playlist link and the whole set queues up, with the Excel tracker showing which files are done. Pause and resume apply here too, so a big playlist over a slow connection is a background chore instead of an evening's babysitting.

Why downloading from your own machine matters

The free downloader websites run their traffic through data center servers, and platforms block those constantly, which is why those sites work on Monday and die on Tuesday, wrapped in pop-ups the whole time. VideoDoc downloads from your own home connection, an ordinary residential line doing an ordinary amount of watching, so it just works, with no ads and nothing uploaded anywhere, the same local-first argument behind offline transcription instead of the cloud.

The copyright part, in plain words

Downloading is a tool, and the rules come from the content. Saving your own uploads, material licensed or marked for reuse, and content where the owner permits offline saving is fine. Re-uploading someone else's work, or using downloads to dodge paying for content, is not, and platform terms restrict downloading much of what they host. Public videos only, and when in doubt, ask the owner. VideoDoc does not touch private videos behind logins unless you point it at your own logged-in browser.

The trick most people miss

You do not have to choose between the file and the knowledge. I often run the same link twice: once as a download for the archive, once as a PDF with the slides included so the content is searchable. The video for rewatching, the PDF for finding, filed together. Quick questions

Can it download whole playlists in HD?

Yes. Paste the playlist link, pick the quality, and every video queues with a live tracker. 4K works when the source video has 4K available.

Does it work for TikTok and Instagram too?

Yes. Paste a public link from YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook or X and VideoDoc detects the platform, then you choose download or document.

Is downloading YouTube videos legal?

It depends on the content and the platform's terms. Your own videos, permitted and licensed material are the safe ground. Re-uploading or avoiding payment for content is not okay, and much platform content restricts downloading, so when in doubt, ask the owner.
