How to turn your own GTA 6 gameplay video into a written guide

Inam Ul Haq·Updated November 9, 2026·9 min read
The short answer

Play and record your own walkthrough once GTA 6 launches on 19 November, and turn that file into spoken text with time stamps and matching screenshots. Use those two outputs as your raw notes to write each step yourself, taking one question at a time. I built VideoDoc to generate those transcripts and image files locally on your own computer, but the tool cannot write your article and it has no knowledge of the game.

When GTA 6 launches on 19 November, you might plan to upload gameplay clips right away. But many players searching online during launch week just want quick text they can read while their game stays on pause: where to go, how to finish an objective, or what they missed. A simple written walkthrough solves that problem, and you can draft it directly from the gameplay recording you already planned to take.

On 9 November, ten days ahead of release, I ran a search for gta 6 guide and found standard news recaps alongside sites claiming to offer complete mission walkthroughs for a title that is not available yet. Those guides are completely fabricated. Do not write a guide until you have played that part yourself. A walkthrough written from your own recorded run gives readers accurate steps that those early placeholder sites cannot match.

Why write a guide instead of only posting clips?

You can see this line drawn clearly on YouTube's own help page for video game content. If you do not hold an explicit licence from the publisher, your gameplay footage must remain minimal, unless your spoken walkthrough directly references the visuals on screen and provides instructional value. The document adds that videos featuring extended, unedited gameplay runs might not qualify for ad monetization. A written walkthrough gives you that instructional foundation, and turning your clip into text takes very little extra effort.

Rockstar sets a tighter standard than YouTube, so take a minute to review their rules before you count on ad revenue. In its policy, non-commercial use is strictly defined as "you don't make money through the game footage you post", and that restriction also bars using gameplay assets to market a product or commercial service. The same terms state that community allowances do not cover digital publishing. There is no custom exception listed for GTA 6.

Because of those written terms, Rockstar has not given you permission to earn from GTA footage. Plenty of existing creators run advertisements on gameplay videos anyway, and the public takedowns I could find were directed at leaks, but unwritten tolerance is not an official grant you can rely on. The original walkthrough you type out belongs entirely to you. But the screenshots you capture are still copyrighted game assets, so keep them minimal and include an image only when text alone cannot explain the step.

Honest limits

VideoDoc does not draft your article, it does not understand GTA 6, and it cannot recognize what a mission objective looks like. It listens to the audio track and picks screens by how much the picture changes. Heavy background music, loud explosions, or weapons fire can drown out your voice and drop words from the text, and English is what it does best. Work exclusively with personal gameplay recorded from a legitimate purchased copy after 19 November. Rockstar states that leaked footage will be pulled down regardless of how the copy was obtained, along with videos of the ending and edited sets of cutscenes.

What is the free way to turn gameplay into a guide?

You do not need to pay for any software to do this, provided you do not mind some manual editing. Speak into your microphone while you play, because a transcription tool can only convert words that were captured on the audio track.

  1. Capture your gameplay using your console's built-in recorder or a capture card, and speak through every choice as you play: which path you take, what failed on your first try, and how you got through.
  2. Open the audio settings and lower the music and ambient sound effects slightly, which keeps your voice clear above the mix.
  3. Post your recording to YouTube under an unlisted privacy setting, give the system time to generate automatic captions, and then open Show transcript beneath the video player to copy the full text and timestamps.
  4. Scroll through your recording to grab still frames for difficult sections, saving screenshots directly from your console gallery or from the paused player.
  5. Draft your walkthrough sections from the generated text, following the steps further down this page.

This no-cost method runs into two practical hurdles. As YouTube says, automatic captions can get words wrong because of accents or background noise, and video games are full of background noise, so plan on correcting names and locations yourself. And this route gives you no pictures at all, so working through a three hour run means pausing and taking screenshots by hand.

How do I turn the transcript into a guide people can follow?

A raw audio transcript reads like rambling speech, so do not publish it unedited. Treat that text as your outline, which is the exact workflow I use when I turn a video into a blog post or convert a YouTube video into a document.

  1. Read the transcript once and mark every time mark where you moved on to a new problem. Those marks are your sections.
  2. Name each section with the question a stuck player would type, then answer it in the first two sentences.
  3. Write the steps in order, in your own words, and leave out everything you only said because you were thinking out loud.
  4. Add a picture only where the words are not enough, such as a turning that is easy to miss.
  5. Put a spoiler warning at the top of any section that gives away the story, and never describe the ending.
  6. Say which console you played on and the date you played, because patches can change things.

You can paste your rough transcript into ChatGPT or Claude and tell the model to clean up your phrasing. But treat the model as a copy editor rather than an author. The language model never played through your session, so it will fill missing details with plausible guesses, and check every step against your own footage before you publish.

Can a GTA 6 guide earn money?

Maybe, but the payouts build slowly and the numbers are small. Before you can monetize video content, your channel must meet the requirements for the YouTube Partner Program, and from 1 February 2027 new channels will need 1,000 subscribers alongside either 8,000 watch hours within a year or 20 million Shorts views within 90 days. No strategy guarantees a result by launch week.

Affiliate commissions offer very little additional income. Under Amazon's rate table, the referral fee is 1% on physical game copies and consoles, and 2% on digital downloads, so an $80 sale pays you just 80 cents at 1%. You also have to say clearly that you earn from the link, and using Rockstar's copyrighted assets to drive sales falls outside their non-commercial policy terms. Operating from Pakistan, I could not find a published rule barring local applicants, but I also found no Amazon page confirming you can join. Treat affiliate links as pocket change, not a plan.

Keep one last safety precaution in mind while looking for software tools or early gameplay footage. Rockstar has not announced a PC edition, and the studio sells pre-orders and not early access, so any site offering early access is a scam. Never download an unverified "PC build" or "leaked copy" to get a head start on your guide.

Quick questions

Can I write a GTA 6 guide before the game comes out?

You cannot write a genuine walkthrough yet. The game launches on 19 November 2026, so any mission guide published ahead of that date is invented. You can use this waiting time to practice recording your commentary and drafting notes from a title you already own.

Does VideoDoc understand what is happening in the game?

No, it does not. The software converts your spoken audio into text and saves pictures when the screen changes. Verifying what those frames mean and ensuring your walkthrough steps work correctly remains your responsibility.

Can I make a guide from someone else's GTA 6 video?

I would advise against that. That player recorded the footage and spoke the commentary, so your guide should come from your own play session. You can study other players' videos for your own information, but write your articles from your own hands-on experience.

Is it allowed to earn money from a GTA 6 guide?

Rockstar's official policy defines non-commercial use as not generating income from their game footage, and I found no statement lifting that condition for GTA 6. The original sentences you draft belong to you, but monetizing accompanying screenshots or clips conflicts with their stated rules, so read the full policy yourself before planning any monetization.

Before the 19th arrives, spend ten minutes recording a run of any title currently in your library, speak your thoughts aloud as you play, and draft one short guide section from the audio. That quick test shows you how clearly you must narrate your actions in real time.
